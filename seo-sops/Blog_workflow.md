# SEO Blog Creation Workflow

## Purpose

Use this workflow to create high-quality SEO articles that are useful for readers, competitive in search, aligned with Google's official guidance, and naturally connected to the business or product being promoted.

The goal is not to produce generic long-form content. The goal is to publish articles that:

- Fully satisfy search intent
- Provide original value beyond competing pages
- Follow Google's Search Essentials and helpful content guidance
- Use AI responsibly when AI assists the writing process
- Include useful visuals, internal links, examples, and practical execution steps
- Include verified statistics, data visuals, and relevant videos where they improve trust or comprehension
- Integrate common reader questions and concise answers where they support AI Overviews, generative search, and human readability
- Integrate the product or service only where it helps the reader complete the task

## Mandatory Google Standards

Every article must be checked against these official Google Search Central resources before publishing:

- [Optimizing your website for generative AI features on Google Search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)
- [Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- [Google Search Essentials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials)
- [Google Search's guidance on generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)
- [Visual Elements gallery of Google Search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/visual-elements-gallery)

### Google Compliance Rules

Use these rules throughout the workflow:

- Write for people first, not only for rankings.
- Add original information, examples, analysis, experience, or perspective.
- Do not simply rewrite competitor pages.
- Avoid scaled-content behavior: do not create many near-duplicate pages just to capture keyword variations.
- Make pages technically eligible for Search: crawlable, indexable where intended, accessible, and compliant with spam policies.
- Use AI as an assistant, not as an unchecked publisher. Human review is required.
- Make AI-assisted content accurate, useful, original, and clearly aligned with the audience's needs.
- Include high-quality relevant images or videos when they help the reader understand or complete the task.
- Verify statistical claims and never invent numbers for charts, screenshots, or data visuals.
- Optimize visible search elements: title link, snippet, attribution, breadcrumbs, images, and rich-result opportunities where relevant.
- Do not rely on unsupported AI SEO hacks such as writing only for "GEO," adding unnecessary `llms.txt` for Google Search, or stuffing long-tail variations.

# Phase 1: Topic Intake

## Step 1.1: Collect Core Inputs

Before research or writing, collect:

- Topic
- Primary keyword
- Secondary keywords
- Target URL or slug
- Topic cluster or parent hub
- Target audience
- Target country or location
- Business or website name
- Website URL
- Product or service to mention
- Desired CTA
- Preferred word-count range
- Existing topical authority roadmap
- Existing internal pages to link
- Competitor URLs, if available
- Required sources or official guidelines
- Required images, screenshots, or product visuals
- Required statistics, datasets, or data sources
- Required YouTube videos or video sources
- Required common questions, if already known
- Claims, products, tools, or examples that must be included
- Claims or subjects that must be avoided

Use this intake format:

```text
Topic:
Primary keyword:
Secondary keywords:
Target URL:
Topic cluster:
Audience:
Target location:
Website:
Product/service:
CTA:
Preferred word count:
Competitor URLs:
Internal pages:
Required sources:
Required visuals:
Required statistics/data:
Required videos:
Required common questions:
Mandatory inclusions:
Topics to avoid:
```

## Step 1.2: Validate the Inputs

Check whether:

- The topic is specific enough to write a useful article.
- The primary keyword matches the topic.
- Secondary keywords support the same intent.
- The audience has a clear problem, task, or decision.
- The target location affects examples, terms, regulations, or tools.
- The business has a natural reason to publish the article.
- The product or service can be included without forcing a sales pitch.
- The article belongs to the correct topical cluster.
- The article will not cannibalize an existing page.

If the keyword, URL, or angle is weak, suggest improvements before writing.

# Phase 2: Intent Mapping

## Step 2.1: Detect Search Intent

Classify the primary intent:

- Informational
- Commercial investigation
- Transactional
- Navigational
- Local
- Problem-solving
- Comparison
- Tool-seeking
- Template-seeking
- Research-seeking

## Step 2.2: Detect Format Intent

Classify the expected article format:

- Listicle
- Step-by-step guide
- Complete guide
- Checklist
- Template article
- Comparison article
- Troubleshooting guide
- Strategy article
- Research article
- Case study
- Tool-led article
- Local service guide
- Decision guide

## Step 2.3: Define the Reader Outcome

Write one sentence:

```text
After reading this article, the reader should be able to:
```

Examples:

- Choose the right tool.
- Complete a process.
- Diagnose a problem.
- Build a checklist.
- Compare options.
- Prepare a client report.
- Decide whether to use a service.

## Step 2.4: Define Reader Sophistication

Classify the reader:

- Beginner
- Intermediate
- Advanced
- Expert
- Mixed audience

Then define how much explanation is needed. Beginner articles need definitions and examples. Advanced articles need specificity, edge cases, and evidence.

# Phase 3: Editorial Path

## Step 3.1: Choose a Path

Choose one:

| Path | Best For | Style |
|---|---|---|
| Semrush Path | Broad informational topics, tutorials, checklists, technical guides, service explainers | Comprehensive, instructional, screenshot-led, highly structured |
| Ahrefs Path | Original research, experiments, case studies, strategic arguments, market opportunities | Evidence-heavy, analytical, opinionated, data-supported |
| Hybrid Path | Topics that need broad keyword coverage plus original examples or strategy | Comprehensive guide with evidence-led sections |

Default path:

```text
Hybrid Path: Semrush-style structure with Ahrefs-style evidence and original insight.
```

## Step 3.2: User Confirmation

When useful, present:

```text
Recommended path:
Reason:
Alternative:
Please choose:
1. Semrush Path
2. Ahrefs Path
3. Hybrid Path
```

If the user does not choose, use the default Hybrid Path.

# Phase 5: Source and Evidence Plan

## Step 5.1: Prioritize Primary Sources

Use primary sources first:

- Google Search Central documentation
- Official product documentation
- Government or regulatory pages
- Standards organizations
- Academic research
- Company case studies with transparent methodology
- Original datasets
- First-party product or client data when available

## Step 5.2: Required Google Source Checks

For SEO articles, check the article plan against the Google guides:

| Google Guide | Workflow Use |
|---|---|
| AI optimization guide | Add AI search readiness only where relevant; keep foundational SEO first |
| Helpful content guide | Ensure original value, completeness, usefulness, and people-first framing |
| Search Essentials | Confirm technical eligibility, spam-policy safety, links, titles, alt text, and structured data basics |
| Generative AI content guidance | Confirm AI-assisted content is accurate, useful, reviewed, and not mass-produced for ranking manipulation |
| Visual Elements gallery | Plan titles, snippets, images, rich results, breadcrumbs, and other search-result appearance opportunities |

## Step 5.3: Verify Important Claims

Verify:

- Statistics
- Dates
- Product capabilities
- Technical SEO recommendations
- Performance thresholds
- Legal or regulatory claims
- Pricing
- Market data
- Ranking or traffic claims

If a claim cannot be verified, soften it or remove it.

## Step 5.4: Plan Statistics and Data

For every article, decide whether statistics would improve trust, context, or decision-making.

Use statistics when they help explain:

- Market size or trend
- Search behavior
- Traffic or click patterns
- Conversion impact
- Technical performance benchmarks
- Adoption rates
- Time savings
- Cost ranges
- Risk or error frequency
- Before-and-after results

Do not add statistics only to make the article look authoritative.

For each statistic, capture:

| Statistic | Source | Date | Where It Fits | Why It Matters | Visual Needed? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |  |  |

Rules:

- Use primary or reputable sources.
- Prefer recent data for fast-changing topics.
- Include the source and date in the article or source notes.
- Do not invent numbers.
- Do not cite a statistic if the methodology is unclear and the claim is important.
- Do not repeat a statistic without adding interpretation.

## Step 5.5: Plan Statistical Images and Data Visuals

Create a statistical image only when a visual makes the data easier to understand.

Good uses:

- Trend chart
- Bar chart
- Before/after comparison
- Benchmark visual
- Funnel chart
- Distribution chart
- Simple scorecard
- Mini dashboard

Avoid:

- Decorative charts with no source
- Invented numbers
- Overloaded infographics
- Tiny unreadable labels
- Charts that repeat a simple sentence

For each data visual, create:

```text
Data visual name:
Data source:
Data date:
Chart type:
Main insight:
Placement:
Alt text:
Caption:
```

If real data is not available, use a conceptual diagram instead of a fake chart.

## Step 5.6: Plan YouTube Video Enrichment

For every article, decide whether a relevant YouTube video would help the reader.

Use videos when they:

- Demonstrate a process
- Explain a technical concept visually
- Show a tool workflow
- Provide expert commentary
- Support a tutorial step
- Help readers who prefer visual learning

Do not add videos just to increase time on page.

Video selection rules:

- Prefer official sources first, such as Google Search Central, product channels, standards organizations, or the brand's own channel.
- Use recent videos for topics that change quickly.
- Check that the video directly supports the surrounding section.
- Avoid embedding competitor sales videos unless the article is a neutral comparison and the video is necessary.
- Avoid low-quality, outdated, misleading, or overly promotional videos.
- Add a short sentence explaining why the video is included.

For each video, capture:

| Video Title | Channel | URL | Publish Date | Placement | Why It Fits |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |  |  |

## Step 5.7: Research Common Questions and AI-Answer Opportunities

For every article, research common questions before drafting.

Sources to review:

- Google People Also Ask
- Related searches
- Search Console queries, if available
- Competitor headings and FAQ sections
- Reddit, Quora, forums, YouTube comments, or community discussions where relevant
- Customer support, sales, or product questions
- AI-style prompts people may ask in ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, or Google AI Overviews

Capture questions in this table:

| Question | Source | Intent | Competitor Answer Pattern | Our Better Answer | Placement |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |  |  |

## Step 5.8: Model Competitor Answers

For each important question, review how competitors answer it:

- Do they answer directly?
- Do they bury the answer?
- Is the answer too generic?
- Is the answer missing steps, examples, data, or caveats?
- Is the answer formatted as a paragraph, list, table, or FAQ?
- Does the answer satisfy the question completely?
- Does the answer create a natural next step?

Then write a better answer that is:

- Direct
- Accurate
- Helpful
- Concise enough to be extracted
- Specific enough to add value
- Supported by the article's broader explanation
- Written naturally for humans, not just AI systems

## Step 5.9: Place Common Questions Inside the Article

Do not automatically create a generic FAQ section. Place questions where they help the reader most.

Good placements:

- H2 or H3 sections when the question is central to the article
- Short answer blocks immediately before deeper explanations
- Tables when readers compare options
- Troubleshooting sections when questions are problem-based
- FAQ section only when several questions do not fit naturally elsewhere

Recommended answer pattern:

```text
Question-style heading
Direct answer in 40-80 words.
Then expand with steps, examples, evidence, visuals, or internal links.
```

AI Overview readiness rules:

- Answer the question clearly near the start of the section.
- Avoid vague intros before the answer.
- Use natural language that matches how people ask questions.
- Include the conditions, caveats, or context that make the answer accurate.
- Do not over-optimize by stuffing many question variations.
- Ensure the answer is supported by the full article.

# Phase 7: Expert Outline

## Step 7.1: Required Outline Elements

Every outline must include:

- H1
- Search intent note or reader context
- Direct answer or quick summary near the top
- Table, checklist, or process overview near the top when useful
- Main sections based on SERP teardown and gap map
- Tactical substeps
- Common questions and concise answer sections
- Examples
- Visual placements
- Statistical data points and data visual placements
- YouTube video placements where relevant
- Internal links
- External/source references
- Product moments
- CTA

## Step 7.2: Section Framework

Each major section should answer:

1. What is it?
2. Why does it matter?
3. How do you do it?
4. What should the reader look for?
5. What mistakes should they avoid?
6. What example makes this concrete?
7. What common question does this section answer?
8. What tool, source, screenshot, data visual, video, or visual supports it?
9. What internal link helps the reader go deeper?

## Step 7.3: Recommended Structures

For a step-by-step guide:

1. Quick answer
2. Process table
3. Prerequisites
4. Numbered steps
5. Common questions answered in relevant sections
6. Examples
7. Mistakes
8. Checklist
9. Product-assisted workflow
10. Final recommendation

For a listicle:

1. Intent framing
2. Quick comparison table
3. Selection criteria
4. Ranked list
5. Best-fit recommendations
6. Common buyer questions answered in relevant sections
7. Evaluation checklist
8. Final recommendation

For a template article:

1. Definition
2. When to use it
3. Template preview
4. Section-by-section breakdown
5. Common usage questions
6. Examples
7. Mistakes
8. Download/copy instructions or CTA

# Phase 8: Visual Plan

## Step 8.1: Plan Visuals Before Writing

Do not add images after the article is finished. Plan them during the outline.

Every visual must help the reader:

- Understand a process
- Complete a task
- Compare options
- Interpret data
- See an interface
- Understand a before/after state
- Use a checklist or framework

Do not add decorative images just to satisfy an image requirement.

## Step 8.2: Preferred Visual Types

Use:

- Real product screenshots when explaining an interface
- Annotated screenshots for tool workflows
- Process diagrams for systems
- Comparison tables for listicles
- Checklists for implementation
- Decision trees for choices
- Charts only when real data exists
- Statistical graphics based on verified data
- Original diagrams when screenshots are unavailable
- Relevant YouTube embeds when video helps the reader complete the task

## Step 8.3: Image Brief Format

For each planned visual, create:

```text
Image name:
Placement:
Purpose:
Type:
Required elements:
Source or production method:
Data source, if applicable:
Alt text:
Caption:
```

## Step 8.4: Visual SEO Rules

Follow Google's image and visual-element guidance:

- Use images that are relevant to the surrounding text.
- Use descriptive filenames.
- Add descriptive alt text.
- Place images near the content they support.
- Avoid text that is too small to read.
- Avoid fake screenshots for real product instructions.
- Compress images for web performance.
- Use structured data where relevant for rich-result eligibility.

## Step 8.5: Video Placement Rules

Place videos:

- After the text introduces the concept
- Before a complex tutorial when the video helps orient the reader
- After a step-by-step section as a supplementary walkthrough
- Near product workflow sections if the video demonstrates the tool

Avoid:

- Stacking multiple videos together
- Adding videos without context
- Embedding videos that replace necessary written instructions
- Using videos as the only explanation of an important step

# Phase 9: Internal Linking Plan

## Step 9.1: Build the Internal-Link Map

Identify:

- Pillar page
- Parent hub
- Sibling posts
- Supporting technical issue pages
- Template pages
- Product pages
- Comparison pages
- Alternative pages
- Reporting pages
- Contact or conversion page

## Step 9.2: Link Placement Rules

Add internal links:

- In the introduction when the hub or pillar is highly relevant
- In sections where the reader needs a deeper explanation
- Around process steps that connect to supporting articles
- Near product or service mentions only when useful
- Near the end for next-step resources

Avoid:

- Repeating the same exact anchor text
- Linking every keyword
- Adding links that do not help the reader
- Using "click here" or vague anchors

## Step 9.3: Target Counts

Use:

- Short article: 4-7 internal links
- Standard article: 7-12 internal links
- Long guide: 12-20 internal links
- Pillar page: 20+ links when natural

# Phase 10: Drafting

## Step 10.1: Draft Section by Section

For each section:

1. State the answer or recommendation.
2. Explain why it matters.
3. Show how to do it.
4. Add a concrete example.
5. Answer the planned common question, if one belongs in the section.
6. Include the planned internal link.
7. Add the source or evidence when needed.
8. Insert the planned visual.
9. Insert planned data visuals or YouTube videos where they support the section.
10. End with a natural transition.

## Step 10.2: Writing Style

Use:

- Clear headings
- Short paragraphs
- Active voice
- Specific nouns
- Concrete examples
- Direct instructions
- Tables for structured comparisons
- Bullets for checklists
- Plain language

Avoid:

- Generic AI phrases
- Keyword stuffing
- Unsupported superlatives
- Decorative fluff
- Long background sections before answering the query
- Rewriting competitors without adding value

## Step 10.3: AI-Assisted Writing Rules

If generative AI is used:

- Human review is required.
- Verify all factual claims.
- Add original examples, product knowledge, or expert perspective.
- Remove generic filler.
- Ensure the article serves the reader, not just the keyword.
- Do not publish AI output as-is.

# Phase 11: Product and CTA Integration

## Step 11.1: Product Integration Rules

Mention the product or service when:

- It helps the reader complete the process
- It supports a practical step
- It provides a relevant screenshot or example
- It reduces manual work
- It clarifies how to implement the advice
- The reader reaches a decision point

Do not mention the product:

- In every section
- Before the reader understands the problem
- As a forced sales pitch
- Without explaining how it helps

## Step 11.2: CTA Rules

Match the CTA to intent:

- Informational: checklist, template, guide, tool-assisted workflow
- Commercial: compare options, view product, try tool, book demo
- Transactional: start trial, request quote, buy, sign up
- Template-seeking: download, copy, generate, customize

# Phase 13: E-E-A-T and Helpful Content Review

## Step 13.1: Helpful Content Gate

Before publishing, answer:

- Does the article provide original information, analysis, examples, or perspective?
- Is it complete enough for the reader's task?
- Does it add value beyond competing pages?
- Would someone bookmark, share, or recommend it?
- Does it avoid exaggeration?
- Is the title descriptive and accurate?
- Is the article written for the target audience, not only for search engines?
- Are common questions answered directly and accurately where they naturally fit?

## Step 13.2: Experience and Trust Gate

Check:

- Author or reviewer information where appropriate
- Publication date and updated date
- Clear source references
- Real examples
- Verified statistics where useful
- Data visuals where useful
- Relevant videos where useful
- First-hand experience where available
- Product screenshots or original visuals where useful
- Transparent limitations
- Accurate business information
- Clear separation between fact, recommendation, and opinion

For high-risk topics, use expert review.
