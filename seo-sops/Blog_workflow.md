Combined SEO Blog Creation Workflow Semrush-First, Ahrefs-Supported
Editorial System Purpose This workflow creates SEO blog content using
one of two editorial paths:

1.  Semrush Path: Comprehensive, instructional, highly structured,
    screenshot-led, and suitable for broad informational keywords.

2.  Ahrefs Path: Research-led, evidence-heavy, strategic, and suitable
    for original insights, case studies, frameworks, and
    opportunity-based articles. The Semrush Path is the recommended
    default. The workflow must first collect the topic and keyword
    information, assess the content opportunity, and then ask the user
    which path they want to follow. Phase 1: Collect the Inputs Step
    1.1: Request Core Inputs Ask the user to provide:

-   Main topic

-   Primary keyword

-   Secondary keywords

-   Target audience

-   Target country or location

-   Business or website name

-   Website URL

-   Product or service to promote

-   Preferred article length

-   Competitor URLs, if available

-   Existing internal pages that should receive links

-   Any claims, products, services, or topics that must be included

-   Any claims or subjects that must be avoided Use this input format:
    Topic: Primary keyword: Secondary keywords: Target audience: Target
    location: Website: Product or service: Preferred word count:
    Competitor URLs: Important internal pages: Mandatory inclusions:
    Topics to avoid: Step 1.2: Validate the Inputs Check whether:

-   The topic is sufficiently specific.

-   The primary keyword matches the topic.

-   The secondary keywords are closely related.

-   The audience has a clear problem or search intent.

-   The target location affects the terminology or examples.

-   The business has a natural reason to publish the article.

-   The article can support the relevant product or service without
    forced promotion. When necessary, suggest improved keyword
    variations before proceeding. Phase 2: Determine Search Intent Step
    2.1: Identify the Primary Intent Classify the keyword as one or more
    of the following:

-   Informational

-   Commercial investigation

-   Transactional

-   Navigational

-   Local

-   Problem-solving

-   Comparison

-   Tool-seeking

-   Template-seeking

-   Research-seeking Step 2.2: Identify the Reader's Desired Outcome
    Define what the reader wants to accomplish. Examples:

-   Understand a concept

-   Fix a problem

-   Compare options

-   Follow a process

-   Calculate something

-   Download a template

-   Evaluate a service

-   Make a purchase decision

-   Find a provider

-   Build a tool

-   Learn a strategy Write the desired outcome in one sentence: After
    reading this article, the reader should be able to: Step 2.3:
    Determine the Content Type Classify the planned article as:

-   Comprehensive guide

-   Step-by-step tutorial

-   Checklist

-   Troubleshooting guide

-   Comparison article

-   Strategy article

-   Research article

-   Case study

-   Original experiment

-   Template article

-   Tool-led article

-   Local service guide

-   Decision guide Phase 3: Recommend the Editorial Path Step 3.1:
    Recommend the Semrush Path When Recommend the Semrush Path when the
    article:

-   Targets a broad informational keyword

-   Requires detailed explanations

-   Needs to cover many subtopics

-   Is aimed at beginners or mixed-experience readers

-   Includes several practical steps

-   Benefits from screenshots or diagrams

-   Has strong People Also Ask opportunities

-   Can act as a topic-cluster hub

-   Needs to rank for many long-tail variations

-   Explains a service, process, system, or technical subject

-   Requires a checklist or implementation framework Examples:

-   What is technical SEO?

-   How does ceramic coating work?

-   Complete guide to dental implants

-   How to choose a commercial fence

-   Website maintenance checklist

-   Common causes of battery failure Step 3.2: Recommend the Ahrefs Path
    When Recommend the Ahrefs Path when the article:

-   Presents an original strategy or viewpoint

-   Uses proprietary data

-   Includes an experiment or case study

-   Compares multiple companies, pages, or methods

-   Demonstrates a market opportunity

-   Uses charts and quantitative evidence

-   Is written for an experienced or specialist audience

-   Challenges common assumptions

-   Requires a claim-evidence-analysis structure

-   Has a narrow topic that benefits from deeper investigation

-   Explains why a strategy works rather than only how to perform it
    Examples:

-   Why free calculators outperform traditional blog posts

-   What we learned from analysing 500 service websites

-   The SEO strategy behind high-traffic local landing pages

-   Why most content audits fail

-   How programmatic pages generated qualified leads Step 3.3: Present
    the Recommendation to the User Use this format: Recommended path:
    Semrush Path Reason: This topic has broad informational intent,
    requires multiple practical sections, and will benefit from
    screenshots, diagrams, and short instructional paragraphs.
    Alternative: The Ahrefs Path would be more suitable if you have
    original data, case-study results, or a strong strategic argument.
    Please choose:

    1.  Semrush Path
    2.  Ahrefs Path
    3.  Hybrid Path Step 3.4: Default Selection When the user does not
        choose a path, use: Default path: Semrush Path with selected
        Ahrefs evidence elements Phase 4: SERP and Competitor Research

Step 4.3: Identify the Content Gap Find at least three types of gaps:
Information gap What questions are not answered? Practical gap What does
the reader still not know how to do? Evidence gap Which claims are
unsupported or weakly supported? Visual gap Which concept would be
easier to understand through a diagram, screenshot, table, or chart?
Decision gap What information is missing when the reader must choose
between options? Phase 5: Build the Article Thesis Step 5.1: Create the
Core Promise Write one sentence defining what the article will deliver.
Example: This guide will explain how technical SEO works, how to
identify common problems, and how to correct them using a practical
step-by-step process. Step 5.2: Create the Unique Value Statement Define
why this article will be better than existing results. Example: Unlike
generic technical SEO checklists, this guide will connect every issue to
its practical impact, show how to identify it, and provide a visual
verification step. Step 5.3: Establish the Main Takeaway Write one
sentence representing the article's central conclusion. Semrush-style
example: Technical SEO is most effective when crawling, indexing, page
performance, and site architecture are reviewed as one connected system.
Ahrefs-style example: Free interactive tools can attract durable organic
traffic because they satisfy task-based intent that ordinary articles
cannot fully replace. Phase 6: Create the Content Outline Semrush Path
Outline Use this structure for the preferred Semrush Path. Step 6.1:
Opening Section Include:

-   Direct definition or answer

-   Why the topic matters

-   What the guide covers

-   Optional short table of contents

-   One opening visual where useful Recommended introduction length:

-   120 to 220 words

-   Three to six short paragraphs

-   Each paragraph should contain one clear idea Step 6.2: Foundational
    Sections Explain:

-   What the topic is

-   How it works

-   Why it matters

-   Who it affects

-   Important terminology

-   Common misconceptions Each foundational section should contain:

-   A direct answer

-   A brief explanation

-   One example

-   One internal link where relevant

-   One visual when the concept is difficult to understand Step 6.3:
    Main Step-by-Step Sections Break the process into numbered actions.
    Example:

    1.  Audit the current website structure
    2.  Check crawl accessibility
    3.  Review indexing signals
    4.  Improve page speed
    5.  Fix duplicate content
    6.  Add structured data
    7.  Monitor performance Each step should contain:

1.  What the step means

2.  Why it matters

3.  How to perform it

4.  What tool or report to use

5.  What the reader should look for

6.  Common mistakes

7.  A visual prompt

8.  A relevant internal link

9.  A practical outcome Step 6.4: Best-Practice Sections Use H3 headings
    for separate recommendations. Recommended section pattern: \###
    Improve the Site Architecture A short definition. Why the issue
    matters. A step-by-step explanation. A screenshot, diagram, or
    example. A short warning or mistake to avoid. A contextual internal
    link. Step 6.5: Troubleshooting Section Include:

Step 6.6: Checklist Section Provide a concise summary checklist near the
end. The checklist should not repeat full explanations. It should
support implementation and review. Step 6.7: Conclusion The conclusion
should:

-   Restate the main takeaway

-   Summarise the highest-priority actions

-   Help the reader identify the next step

-   Introduce the business or service naturally

-   Avoid a sudden hard sell Recommended length:

-   120 to 200 words Ahrefs Path Outline Use this structure when the
    user selects the Ahrefs Path. Step 6.8: Opening Claim Start with:

-   A surprising observation

-   A contradiction

-   An emerging opportunity

-   A real result

-   A strong practical problem Avoid beginning with a generic
    definition. Example: Most businesses continue investing in blog
    posts even when their audience is searching for a tool, calculator,
    or template instead. Step 6.9: Present the Evidence Include:

-   Original data

-   Analytics data

-   Search-volume data

-   Traffic estimates

-   Case-study results

-   Named examples

-   Before-and-after comparisons Use the pattern: Claim → Example →
    Evidence → Interpretation Step 6.10: Group the Opportunity Divide
    the strategy into clear categories. Examples:

-   Calculators

-   Converters

-   Generators

-   Templates Or:

-   Low-cost opportunities

-   Mid-market opportunities

-   Enterprise opportunities Or:

-   Informational pages

-   Commercial pages

-   Tool pages

-   Local pages Use the same internal structure for each category. Step
    6.11: Repeat the Evidence Framework For every category:

1.  Explain the category

2.  Present one strong example

3.  Present one relevant industry example

4.  Show quantitative evidence

5.  Explain why it works

6.  Identify the practical opportunity

7.  Include a table or chart

8.  Link to supporting research Step 6.12: Provide the Execution
    Framework End with a clear method such as:

9.  Identify the opportunity

10. Validate the demand

11. Filter unrealistic targets

12. Review the SERP

13. Build the asset

14. Publish it

15. Promote it

16. Measure the results

17. Improve it Step 6.13: Close With an Action-Oriented Conclusion The
    conclusion should reinforce the opportunity and encourage
    implementation. Avoid repeating every example. Hybrid Path Outline
    Use the Hybrid Path when:

-   The topic requires a comprehensive guide

-   Original data or case studies are also available

-   The article should rank broadly but still present an original
    argument Recommended structure:

1.  Direct Semrush-style definition

2.  Ahrefs-style central thesis

3.  Semrush-style foundational explanation

4.  Ahrefs-style evidence section

5.  Semrush-style numbered implementation steps

6.  Ahrefs-style examples and comparison tables

7.  Semrush-style troubleshooting and checklist

8.  Natural service or product CTA Phase 7: Paragraph and Readability
    Rules Step 7.1: Semrush Path Paragraph Rules Use:

-   15 to 45 words per paragraph

-   One to three sentences per paragraph

-   One clear point per paragraph

-   Short transition paragraphs

-   Lists for multi-step instructions

-   Tables for comparisons

-   Notes for important warnings Avoid:

-   Large text blocks

-   Repeating the heading in the first sentence

-   One-sentence paragraphs used too frequently

-   Excessive fragments

-   Unnecessary background information Step 7.2: Ahrefs Path Paragraph
    Rules Use:

-   35 to 80 words per paragraph

-   Two to four sentences per paragraph

-   A claim, example, and implication where suitable

-   Short emphasis paragraphs between analytical sections

-   More detailed interpretation of evidence Avoid:

-   Data without interpretation

-   Examples without strategic relevance

-   Long introductions before the main insight

-   Repeating metrics in text and tables without adding analysis Step
    7.3: Sentence Rules Prefer:

-   Active voice

-   Direct instructions

-   Specific nouns

-   Concrete examples

-   Measurable outcomes

-   Natural transitions Avoid:

-   Unnecessary jargon

-   Artificially polished language

-   Repetitive sentence patterns

-   Generic AI phrases

-   Empty claims such as "in today's digital landscape"

-   Unsupported superlatives

-   Keyword stuffing Phase 8: Image Planning Step 8.1: Assign a Purpose
    to Every Image Every image must perform one of these functions:

-   Explain a process

-   Demonstrate a tool

-   Show evidence

-   Compare options

-   Summarise a framework

-   Illustrate a system

-   Present a before-and-after state

-   Help the reader complete a task Do not add images only for
    decoration. Step 8.2: Recommended Image Types Semrush-style images
    Use:

-   Annotated screenshots

-   Process diagrams

-   Flowcharts

-   Site architecture diagrams

-   Before-and-after examples

-   Interface walkthroughs

-   Error examples

-   Checklist graphics

-   Decision trees

-   SERP screenshots Ahrefs-style images Use:

-   Traffic graphs

-   Performance charts

-   Keyword tables

-   Comparison tables

-   Case-study visuals

-   Data summaries

-   Trend charts

-   Opportunity matrices

-   Product-result screenshots Step 8.3: Image Placement Rules Place an
    image:

-   Immediately after the concept it supports

-   Immediately after an instruction that refers to an interface

-   Before a detailed explanation when the diagram provides necessary
    context

-   After a claim when the image contains proof

-   Before a comparison paragraph when the reader needs the table first
    Avoid placing several images together without interpretation. Step
    8.4: Image Frequency For a Semrush-style guide:

-   One visual every 250 to 450 words

-   One screenshot for each important tool-based action

-   One diagram for each difficult system

-   One summary table for each comparison-heavy section For an
    Ahrefs-style article:

-   One evidence visual for each major claim

-   One chart or table for each category

-   One workflow screenshot for each tool-based execution step Phase 9:
    Image Prompt Creation Step 9.1: Universal Image Prompt Formula Use
    this formula: Create a clean editorial illustration for an SEO blog
    about \[TOPIC\]. Purpose: \[Explain what the image must help the
    reader understand.\] Show: \[List the required elements.\] Layout:
    \[Flowchart, comparison, process diagram, dashboard, cutaway,
    timeline, or annotated interface.\] Visual style: Professional SaaS
    editorial design, minimal, modern, clear hierarchy, high
    readability, restrained use of colour, simple icons, generous
    spacing. Text requirements: Use only the following short labels:
    \[LIST LABELS\] Avoid: Stock-photo appearance, decorative elements,
    excessive gradients, tiny text, visual clutter, fake company logos,
    watermarks, unrealistic interface details. Aspect ratio: 16:9
    landscape. Output: High-resolution blog image with enough empty
    space around the edges for responsive cropping. Step 9.2: Process
    Diagram Prompt Create a clean editorial process diagram explaining
    \[PROCESS\]. Show the process from left to right:

    1.  \[STEP ONE\]
    2.  \[STEP TWO\]
    3.  \[STEP THREE\]
    4.  \[STEP FOUR\]
    5.  \[STEP FIVE\] Connect each stage with simple arrows. Use a
        professional SEO software blog style with minimal icons, a light
        background, clear labels, strong visual hierarchy, and generous
        spacing. Use only the listed step names as visible text. Avoid
        decorative illustrations, stock imagery, gradients, small text,
        and unnecessary detail. Aspect ratio: 16:9. Step 9.3: Site
        Architecture Prompt Create a clear website architecture diagram
        for an SEO guide. Show:

    -   Homepage at the top
    -   Main category pages below it
    -   Service or product pages below each category
    -   Supporting blog articles connected to the most relevant category
    -   Internal links between closely related pages Use a clean
        hierarchy with boxes and connecting lines. Highlight the
        difference between a logical shallow structure and a confusing
        deep structure. Professional technical SEO editorial style,
        white background, restrained colours, readable labels, no
        decorative elements. Aspect ratio: 16:9. Step 9.4:
        Before-and-After Prompt Create a split-screen before-and-after
        SEO illustration. Left side: \[Describe the problem.\] Right
        side: \[Describe the improved result.\] Use identical page
        structures on both sides so the differences are easy to compare.
        Add short labels: Before After Professional editorial diagram,
        clean layout, minimal icons, high readability, white background,
        no stock photography. Aspect ratio: 16:9. Step 9.5: Decision
        Tree Prompt Create a practical decision-tree diagram for readers
        deciding \[DECISION\]. Start with: \[STARTING QUESTION\]
        Branches:
    -   If \[CONDITION\], recommend \[ACTION\].
    -   If \[CONDITION\], recommend \[ACTION\].
    -   If \[CONDITION\], recommend \[ACTION\]. End each branch with one
        clear next step. Use simple boxes, arrows, and short labels.
        Professional SEO blog design, uncluttered, highly readable,
        accessible contrast, white background. Aspect ratio: 16:9. Step
        9.6: Checklist Image Prompt Create a concise visual checklist
        for \[TOPIC\]. Include these checklist items:
    -   \[ITEM ONE\]
    -   \[ITEM TWO\]
    -   \[ITEM THREE\]
    -   \[ITEM FOUR\]
    -   \[ITEM FIVE\]
    -   \[ITEM SIX\] Use check icons and group related actions into
        clear sections. Professional editorial infographic, clean grid,
        generous spacing, short readable labels, no decorative
        photography. Aspect ratio: 4:5 or 1:1. Step 9.7: Data Chart
        Prompt Use image generation only when the chart is conceptual.
        For real data, generate the chart from verified numbers rather
        than asking an image model to invent it. Conceptual prompt:
        Create a conceptual editorial chart showing the relationship
        between \[METRIC ONE\] and \[METRIC TWO\]. The chart should
        communicate: \[INSIGHT\] Do not display invented numerical
        values. Use labels such as: Low Medium High Increasing
        opportunity Increasing competition Professional SEO research
        style, minimal, readable, no decorative elements. Aspect ratio:
        16:9. Step 9.8: Annotated Screenshot Brief Do not create fake
        screenshots when a real interface is being taught. Use this
        production brief: Capture the real screen showing \[REPORT OR
        FEATURE\]. Annotate:

    1.  Highlight \[ELEMENT\].
    2.  Add arrow pointing to \[ELEMENT\].
    3.  Add a short label explaining \[ACTION\].
    4.  Blur private or client-specific information.
    5.  Crop unrelated navigation.
    6.  Preserve enough interface context for orientation. Export as
        WebP at twice the intended display width. Phase 10: Internal
        Linking Step 10.1: Build the Internal-Link Map Identify:

-   Primary service page

-   Related service pages

-   Relevant category pages

-   Supporting blog posts

-   Product pages

-   Location pages

-   Contact or consultation page

-   Useful tools or resources Step 10.2: Semrush Path Linking Rules For
    a comprehensive guide:

-   Add one internal link in the introduction when highly relevant

-   Add one to two contextual links in each major section

-   Add deeper supporting guides after complex subsections

-   Link service pages where the reader needs professional help

-   Add a related-reading section near the end

-   Avoid linking the same page repeatedly without a clear reason
    Typical target:

-   7 to 15 useful internal links, depending on article length Step
    10.3: Ahrefs Path Linking Rules For a research-led article:

-   Keep the analytical paragraphs relatively clean

-   Add product links during execution steps

-   Add supporting research after major sections

-   Add further-reading links after categories

-   Link methodology pages where metrics are discussed Typical target:

-   5 to 10 strong internal links Step 10.4: Anchor-Text Rules Use
    descriptive anchors such as:

-   technical SEO audit

-   commercial fence installation

-   page-speed optimisation guide

-   dental implant consultation

-   keyword research process Avoid:

-   Click here

-   Read more

-   Learn more, unless the surrounding text makes the destination
    completely clear

-   Exact-match keyword repetition in every link

-   Unnatural promotional anchors Phase 11: External Sources and
    Evidence Step 11.1: Prioritise Primary Sources Use:

-   Government websites

-   Standards organisations

-   Academic research

-   Official product documentation

-   Industry associations

-   Original datasets

-   Company case studies with transparent methodology Step 11.2: Verify
    Every Important Claim Verify:

-   Statistics

-   Legal or regulatory requirements

-   Product capabilities

-   Technical recommendations

-   Performance thresholds

-   Health or safety claims

-   Costs

-   Dates

-   Market data Step 11.3: Ahrefs Evidence Pattern For every major
    strategic claim, aim to include:

-   One named example

-   One verified data point

-   One visual

-   One interpretation

-   One practical implication Do not assume that a chart speaks for
    itself. Phase 12: Tables and Structured Elements Step 12.1: Use
    Tables When Use a table for:

-   Comparisons

-   Troubleshooting

-   Decision criteria

-   Costs

-   Features

-   Pros and cons

-   Keyword opportunities

-   Process summaries

-   Before-and-after changes

-   Tool recommendations Step 12.2: Table Rules Each table must:

-   Have a clear purpose

-   Use short column labels

-   Contain comparable information

-   Be explained before or after

-   Avoid excessively long text

-   Remain readable on mobile Step 12.3: Suggested Tables For
    Semrush-style content:

-   Problem, cause, fix

-   Step, tool, outcome

-   Method, benefit, limitation

-   Element, recommendation, reason For Ahrefs-style content:

-   Page, keyword, traffic, difficulty

-   Example, strategy, result

-   Opportunity, demand, competition

-   Category, use case, business value Phase 13: Draft the Article Step
    13.1: Draft Section by Section For every section:

1.  State the answer or recommendation

2.  Explain why it matters

3.  Add an example

4.  Provide the practical action

5.  Add supporting evidence where required

6.  Add the planned internal link

7.  Insert the visual brief
